About

I work on the boundary between software and hardware, where a program's performance depends on how a compiler maps it onto the machine that runs it.

I am a Software Engineer in the R&D division at WSO2 and an elected Project Management Committee member of Apache Synapse, where I lead security and serve as release manager. In July 2026 I completed a B.Sc. Engineering (Hons) in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Moratuwa, graduating with First Class Honours and a CGPA of 3.88/4.00.

My undergraduate thesis, NUMA-Torch, extended PyTorch's TorchInductor compiler to make large language model prefill NUMA-aware on multi-socket CPUs, reaching up to 2.63× prefill speedup with no changes to model code; it appears at HiPC 2026. Alongside it I have published first-author work on temporal action localization and studied noise-robust biometric identification. I am currently continuing part-time research on compilers for sparse computations with my thesis advisors.

My research interests lie in compilers and computer architecture — in particular, compilation for specialized and reconfigurable hardware, dynamically scheduled dataflow architectures, and performance engineering for machine learning systems. What draws me to these problems is that performance there is decided by how a compiler reasons about the machine, not by how fast the machine is.
